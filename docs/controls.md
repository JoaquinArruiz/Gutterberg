# Controls

| Action | Input |
| --- | --- |
| Select / Grid / Piece / Pan tool | `V` / `G` / `C` / `H` (or hold `Space` to pan temporarily, or middle-mouse drag). A PDF opens with the Grid tool, and drawing a grid switches to the Select tool with the grid picked (Preferences › Display › Tools turns that off) |
| Select tool | Press any grid or piece on the page to pick it and drag it; the picked one's handles resize it (and turn a piece). Pieces come first where they lie over a grid. `Shift`-click adds a piece to the picked ones, `Alt`-drag drags copies, and the arrow keys, `Delete` and `Ctrl/Cmd+D` act on the picked pieces, or on the picked grid when no piece is picked. A press on the empty page picks nothing and draws nothing: the Grid and Piece tools draw |
| Draw selection | drag on the page; drag the body to move, the handles to resize; click empty page to clear. With several grids on the page this is the active grid; the others are drawn dimmer. Pressing the region picks it: the arrow keys then move it 1 mm (0.05 in with inches), `Shift`+arrows 10 mm (0.5 in), a burst of presses being one undo step; `Esc` unpicks it |
| Turn a grid into pieces (Source tab) | Source sidebar › Layout › Turn into pieces: every cell of the viewed page's active grid becomes a freeform piece at the same place and size, and that grid's region is cleared. For several sizes on a page that stay grids, use Add grid instead. If the grid is shared by several pages it asks: this page only, or every page of the group. Copies, turn, size, back and place in the order of each piece are kept. One undo step |
| Several grids on a page | The Grids and pieces panel (under Properties on the right at first; the Panels menu moves or hides it like any panel): one button per grid of the viewed group (Grid 1, Grid 2… or the name it was given, with its rows × columns), Add grid (up to 9) and a bin on every grid (on the last grid it only clears the region). The page's freeform pieces are listed after the grids: pressing one picks it with the Piece tool, and its bin deletes it. The picked grid is the active one: the page, Layout, presets and Apply this grid to… act on it. With the Select or the Grid tool, pressing a dimmer grid on the page makes it active and starts moving it. Each grid's name shows above it on the page. Deleting the last grid only clears its region. Pieces come out grid by grid, row by row, then the page's freeform pieces |
| Right-click menus | On the page (Source tab), whichever tool is on: a piece has Delete (every picked piece when it is one of them), and a grid, the picked one or a dimmer one, has Hide and Delete (Clear the region on the last grid). Pieces come first where they lie over a grid. In the Grids and pieces list: a grid has Rename, Change color, Hide or Show, Move up, Move down, Move to each other folder, Move to a new folder, Take out of its folder and Delete; a folder has Rename, Hide or Show, Move up, Move down and Ungroup (Delete when it is empty); a piece has Delete. Arrow keys move through a menu, Enter picks, `Esc`, a press outside or scrolling closes it |
| Apply a detected grid | Detect on this page is at the bottom of Layout. When the grid being edited already has a region, a grid proposal offers Add as new grid (a new grid after the others, picked; Enter does this) and Replace Grid n (the grid being edited, as before); a proposal of several grids offers Add as new grids and Replace grids. A page with nothing drawn, and freeform pieces, have a single Apply |
| Name, color and folders of grids | In the Grids and pieces panel: double-click a grid's or a folder's name to rename it (Enter or leaving the field keeps it, `Esc` drops it; an empty grid name goes back to Grid n); the dot at the left of a grid opens its colors (Suggested: Default, the accent color, and eight more; Your colors: the ones you added, kept in Preferences for every project; + adds one from the system's picker and gives it to the grid, and a right-click on one of yours, or the × that shows on it, deletes it; a press outside or `Esc` closes them); the pencil has the name, the color and the folder together (No folder, a folder of the page, or New folder). The folder-and-plus button in the panel's header, next to its ⋯ menu, adds an empty folder, listed first and ready to be named. Drag a grid or a folder in the list to change the order (a grid's place is the order its pieces come out), or drag a grid onto a folder to put it in (onto the folder's upper part: before it). The eye hides a grid, or every grid of a folder, on the page: it is not drawn or edited there but still has its pieces and prints. Pressing a folder picks its grids together: dragging one of them on the page, or the arrow keys, moves them all as one, stopping together at the page's edge, without handles to resize. Pressing one grid, in the list or on the page outside the picked folder, picks it alone, even when it is in a folder; `Esc` drops the folder. A folder has a pencil to rename it, a button to take its grids out, and folds away in the list |
| Draw freeform pieces (Piece tool) | drag on the page for each piece; drag a piece to move it, its handles to resize, the dot above it to rotate (`Shift` snaps to 15°); `Delete` removes the picked pieces; the Freeform pieces section in the Source sidebar has their angle and size (a value typed goes to every picked piece) |
| Pick and move several pieces (Piece tool) | `Shift`-click adds a piece to the picked ones or takes it out, `Ctrl/Cmd+A` picks every piece of the page, `Esc` picks none; dragging one of the picked pieces moves them all; the arrow keys move them 1 mm (`Shift`: 10 mm), and with nothing picked the arrows change page |
| Copy pieces (Piece tool) | `Ctrl/Cmd+D` copies the picked pieces 5 mm down and to the right and picks the copies; `Alt`-drag leaves the pieces where they are and drags copies. Copies start with no turn, size, back or copies of their own |
| Snapping | while drawing, dragging or resizing the grid region or dragging or resizing a piece, its edges and middle snap to the page's edges and middle, to the other pieces and the region, and to the guides, with a red guide line; hold `Ctrl/Cmd` to drag without it, or turn it off with the magnet in the toolbar (kept in preferences). A turned piece snaps by its upright box, and only an upright piece snaps while resized. A side that snapped stays until the pointer has moved about 10 pixels on (sticky) |
| Sticky edges | with the sticky edges button in the toolbar (on by default, kept in preferences), the sides of the grid region and of upright pieces also stick to the straight edges in the artwork: piece borders, cut lines, the sides of drawn boxes and pictures, long contrast lines on a scan. An edge only pulls a side it runs along for at least half of its length, so art inside a piece does not. `Ctrl/Cmd` held drags freely. A help tip at the button explains it the first time a side sticks |
| Rulers and guides | the rulers button in the toolbar or `Ctrl/Cmd+R` (off by default, kept in preferences): rulers along the top and left of the page (Original view and the Original side of Split), from the page's top-left corner, in the unit of Preferences, with a mark that follows the pointer. Drag from the top ruler for a horizontal guide, from the left one for a vertical guide (Preferences › Display › Invert rulers swaps them: each ruler then gives the guide across it, and a guide is deleted on the ruler it came from); its distance from the corner shows while dragging. With any tool but Pan, press on a guide to move it, or drag it back onto its ruler to delete it; the corner where the rulers meet has Clear guides. Guides show and are snapped to (with the magnet) while the rulers are on; they are kept for the session, the same on every page of a PDF, and not saved in the project (that would need a new project version, so it waits until someone asks) |
| Zoom | `+` / `-`, toolbar, or `Ctrl/Cmd` + wheel / trackpad pinch (zooms at the cursor) |
| Fit page | `0` |
| Pan | wheel / trackpad scroll (`Shift` + wheel: sideways) |
| Pages | `PageUp`/`PageDown`, or the left and right arrow keys when nothing is picked (with a region or pieces picked they move it) |
| Source \| Print | tabs in the toolbar. Print: piece library (click, Ctrl/Cmd-click, Shift-click; Copies field and steppers), sheet preview (the sheet in front is always live; the tag button in its header shows each piece's reference, such as `p3 · r1 c2`, `p3 · g2 · r1 c2` when the page has several grids, or `A · p3 · r1 c2` with several PDFs, on the sheet, on screen only) with a hideable row of sheet thumbnails (follows Live Preview, with a refresh button when it is off), and the print settings with Plan, Sheet and Page sections that fold (and remember it) |
| Move the panels | Panels menu in the toolbar, a panel's own menu, or Preferences › Layout: put each panel on the left, right, top or bottom, or hide it. The Source tab has Pages, Properties and Grids and pieces around the page; the Print tab has the Piece library and the Print settings around the sheets. Each tab keeps its own layout and sizes, and the Print settings only go left or right |
| Turn, size and sort pieces (Print tab) | piece library: `R` / `Shift+R` turn the selection 90° right / left, "Make all portrait / landscape"; drag pieces to reorder them (the sheets follow); the Selected pieces section of the print settings sets a real size (e.g. 63 × 88 mm), a percentage or a size preset (the common sleeve sizes, or sizes you save under a name, fixing both sides or only the width or the height), and scaled pieces carry a badge |
| Backs (Print tab) | Print settings › Duplex: Print backs, then Backs: On the other side (duplex: flip edge and back offset; back sheets have no cut marks, since you cut through both sides from the front, unless Print settings › Cut marks › Also on back sheets is on, for backs printed on their own sheets and cut or glued separately; Calibration sheet… saves a front and a back to print on both sides of one sheet of paper, to hold up to a light and read the offset on the rulers: the number where the front's lines cross the horizontal ruler is X, the vertical one Y) or Beside the front (gutterfold: Pairs Fold or Cut apart, where the back goes, back turn, gap between front and back; Cut apart draws no fold line and puts cut marks around the front and the back each, for quick builds that are not double-sided). Pick back… in Selected pieces gives the selection a back; Pair backs automatically… pairs every piece at once (Alternating pages, Backs after fronts, Beside each other on the page, or Backs in another PDF, offered when the project has two or more PDFs: pick the PDF with the backs and page n of every other PDF pairs with its page n, piece by piece; a backs PDF that is one single piece can be the common back of every front; with mirrored and upside-down options; pieces without a match stay unpaired and are counted). With AI Mode on there is also By picture, with AI: Find backs with AI sends a small picture of every piece (up to 100) and shows what the answer pairs before Apply; every piece it calls a back stops printing as a front, and only the backs it finds upside down are turned. A back turned in the library prints turned that much more. Gutterfold file to double-sided: mark the pieces, pair them with Beside each other, then Backs: On the other side. Double-sided file to gutterfold: pair with Alternating pages, then Backs: Beside the front |
| Projects | File menu: New project (`Ctrl/Cmd+N`), Open PDF (`Ctrl/Cmd+O`), Open project (`Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+O`), Add PDF, Save (`Ctrl/Cmd+S`), Save as (`Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+S`) and the recent projects. A project is a `.gtr` file with the page groups, freeform pieces, turns, sizes, order, print plan and output settings of every PDF; it points at the PDFs instead of holding them, and asks where a PDF went if it moved |
| Images as pieces | File › Add images… (or the Add images button in the Source tab's page panel, "Start from images" on the start screen, or dropping image files on the window): PNG, JPEG or WebP, one piece per image, the size chosen once in a dialog. An import is one document named after its first image (rename it with the pencil next to the name) |
| Several PDFs | "Add PDF…" in the page panel adds a PDF to the project; the dropdown above the pages switches the PDF being edited. The piece library shows the pieces of all of them (filter by PDF or group), and the Print tab puts them on the same sheets. The Source tab's export is for the PDF being edited |
| Grid presets | Presets section of the Source sidebar: save the viewed group's rows, columns and source gap under a name, apply it to any other page group. Presets belong to you, not to a project |
| Skip a page / include it | checkbox on its thumbnail, or a right-click on it (skipped pages are left out of the export) |
| Remove pages from view | Right-click a page's thumbnail: Remove page, or Remove the other pages to keep only that one. A removed page is skipped and leaves the Pages panel and the arrow keys, so the pages in use are easier to work with; it stays in the PDF and the project, and one page always stays in view (on the last one, or a PDF of one page, both options are off). Removed pages (n) at the bottom of the panel is folded at first: unfolded, it lists them, and clicking one, or a right-click on it, brings it back included; a right-click on the title brings them all back. Apply this grid to… leaves removed pages alone |
| Different grid for some pages | draw the grid on a page, then "Apply this grid to…" (this page, a range, all pages of the same size; with several grids, all of them or only the active one); edits then apply to the group the viewed page belongs to |
| Undo / redo | `Ctrl/Cmd+Z` / `Shift+Ctrl/Cmd+Z` (also `Ctrl+Y`), or the toolbar buttons. One history for everything, including the Print plan (copies and plan settings): it undoes the last change made in either tab; a whole drag is one step |
| Welcome tour and Help | The welcome tour opens by itself once, over the start screen (Skip, `Esc` or Get started close it for good). Replay it from the start screen or Preferences › Help, which also turns the help tips on or off (a tip's own “Don't show tips” does the same) and brings the closed ones back, and lists every shortcut. A tip's “Open Preferences” opens the setting it talks about, which pulses twice. Preferences › About copies the app info (version, system, language, pdfium) or the end of the log, opens the GitHub bug report form with the version and system filled in, and opens the Discord server |
| Help menu and tutorials | The `?` button in the toolbar, next to Preferences, with or without a PDF open: Tutorials…, Keyboard shortcuts, Welcome tour and Report a problem (Preferences › About, on “Found a bug?”). Tutorials… opens a dialog with the tutorials grouped by tab (Source tab, Print tab, Duplex, App) and a search field at the top (by title or summary, ignoring case and accents); Preferences › Help lists the same tutorials above the tips switch. Picking one closes the dialog and Preferences and starts it: a card points at the real control with a spotlight, with Back and Next, and moves on by itself when you do the step. It works with help tips turned off, no tip shows while it runs, and Done, the ×, or `Esc` end it. A tutorial that needs a PDF, or a PDF with pieces, says so first and offers Open PDF… or the “Mark pieces and export” tutorial; it never opens a file or changes the project by itself. Nothing about it is saved |
| Keyboard shortcuts | `?`, or File › Keyboard shortcuts…: every shortcut by area, in the app's language, with a search field at the top (by text or by key, ignoring case and accents) (`src/lib/shortcuts.ts`, kept in step with the code by hand: a shortcut added or changed in a `keydown` handler changes there too) |
| Start screen | With nothing open: Open PDF, Open project and the recent projects |
| Exact values | Source layout section: columns, rows, piece width/height and selection X/Y. Enter commits, Esc reverts, ↑/↓ nudge. Type `63.5` or `63,5`: both work whatever the decimal separator preference says. Every number field also takes arithmetic with `+ - * /` and parentheses (`12/3` gives 4, `(63,5 + 3) * 2` gives 133), worked out on Enter or when the field is left and rounded to the decimals it shows; a sum that does not work out (a typo, a division by zero) puts the old value back |
| Language and numbers | Preferences (`Ctrl/Cmd+,`) › General: Language (System / English / Español) and Decimal separator (Automatic / Dot / Comma) |

Geometry is stored normalized (0..1 of the page, top-left origin); all conversions live in
`src/lib/coordinates.ts` and `src/lib/units.ts`. Frontend unit tests: `pnpm test`.
