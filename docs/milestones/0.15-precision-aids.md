# 0.15.0 — Precision aids: sticky edges and rulers

Two aids for marking pieces exactly, on top of 0.14.0's snapping (`src/lib/snap.ts`, the magnet in the toolbar):

- **Sticky edges:** while a grid region (or a piece) is drawn, moved or resized, its edges stick to the edges in the
  artwork, where the contrast jumps, and stay stuck for a few pixels before letting go. On by default.
- **Rulers and guides:** rulers along the top and left of the page, in the user's unit, with marks that follow the
  pointer; guides dragged out of them, which things snap to. Off by default.

Do the parts in this order; each one is a feature branch of its own, merged into `release/0.15.0`.

1. Finding the edges (Rust)
2. Sticky snapping and its switch
3. Rulers
4. Guides
5. The toolbar group, the help tip, the shortcuts sheet and the docs (**last**)

## Where the switches go

The magnet (snap to the page and the pieces), sticky edges and the rulers are switches that change how editing on
the page behaves, not tools you pick. They go together in their own group in the Source tab's toolbar, after the
tools and separated from them by a divider: magnet, sticky edges (Lucide `Tangent`), rulers (Lucide `Ruler`). Like
the tools, they are hidden when no document is open (0.14.0 part 7). They are not "accessibility" settings in the
usual sense (screen readers, contrast, keyboard); "precision aids" is the word for them in the docs.

## Part 1 — Finding the edges (Rust)

Detect pieces already reads what is needed: `read_page_data` (`crates/card-core/src/detect/read.rs`) gives a page's
objects (drawn rectangles, images) and a grey render, and `detect/edges.rs` measures contrast in it.

1. **`card_core::detect::page_edges`** (or a module next to it): the strong straight edges of a page, as segments in
   points with a top-left origin: vertical ones (`x`, `y0`..`y1`) and horizontal ones (`y`, `x0`..`x1`), each with a
   strength 0..1.
   - From the PDF's objects first: the sides of drawn rectangles and images are exact edges.
   - From the grey render: along every pixel column (and row), the runs where the contrast across it is strong and
     the same sign, at least `MIN_EDGE_MM` long (start around 5 mm), merged when they are within a pixel or two.
     Art inside a piece makes short runs; piece borders and cut lines make long ones.
   - Capped (the strongest few hundred per axis), so the answer stays small.
2. **A Tauri command** (`src-tauri/src/commands/detect.rs`, on the render worker like Detect pieces) that returns
   them for a document's page. The UI asks once per page and keeps the answer in memory for the session.
3. **Tests** in `crates/card-core/tests/`: a page with drawn rectangles gives their sides; a grey image with a dark
   rectangle on white gives its four sides and nothing for a noisy patch smaller than `MIN_EDGE_MM`; the cap holds.

## Part 2 — Sticky snapping and its switch

1. **Edges as targets.** `src/lib/snap.ts` takes the page's edges as extra targets, converted to the page's
   normalized coordinates. An edge counts for a moving line only where they overlap: a vertical edge snaps a
   region's left or right side if it runs along at least half of that side, so art inside a piece never pulls a
   border.
2. **Sticky.** Today a line snaps within `SNAP_PX` and lets go as soon as it is further away. Make it stick: once a
   line has snapped, it stays until the pointer has moved `RELEASE_PX` (start around 10 screen px) away from where
   it snapped, then follows the pointer again. Hysteresis in pure functions, tested; it applies to every target
   (page, pieces, edges, guides) so snapping feels the same everywhere.
3. **The switch.** "Stick to edges in the artwork" (Lucide `Tangent`), on by default, stored as `editor.snapEdges`
   in **preferences version 15**. Off: the edges are not targets; the magnet still decides the page and the pieces.
   `Ctrl/Cmd` held during a drag still turns all snapping off.
4. **What it applies to:** drawing, moving and resizing the grid region; moving and resizing upright pieces (a turned
   piece is not along the page's axes, as in 0.14.0).
5. **Tests** for the overlap rule, the hysteresis, and a viewport test where a region edge sticks to an edge and
   lets go after `RELEASE_PX`.

## Part 3 — Rulers

1. **The switch.** "Rulers" (Lucide `Ruler`), off by default, `editor.rulers` in preferences version 15. Optionally
   `Ctrl/Cmd+R` too (check that the web view's own reload is not in the way; 0.9.3 turned its shortcuts off).
2. **The rulers.** A strip along the top and one along the left of the page canvas (the Original view, and the
   Original side of Split), measuring from the page's top-left corner, in the user's unit (mm, cm, in). The ticks
   follow the zoom: the step is the smallest of 1, 5, 10, 50 mm (or 1/16, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, 1 in) whose ticks stay a
   readable distance apart, with numbers on the larger ones. Tick maths in `src/lib/rulers.ts`, pure and tested.
3. **Pointer marks.** A thin mark on each ruler at the pointer's position, following it over the page.
4. Light and dark themes; the rulers take no room from the page when they are off.

## Part 4 — Guides

1. **Making one.** Drag from the top ruler to make a horizontal guide, from the left one for a vertical guide. While
   dragging, its position shows next to it in the user's unit.
2. **Changing one.** A guide is grabbed by pressing on it (a few pixels either side) with the Grid region or Piece
   tool; drag to move it, drag it back onto its ruler to delete it. "Clear guides" in a small menu at the corner
   where the rulers meet.
3. **Snapping.** Guides are snap targets for the region and the pieces (with the magnet on), and sticky like the rest.
4. **Kept for the session**, per PDF (the same guides on every page of the PDF being edited), in the editor store; not
   saved in the project and not part of undo. Saving them would need a project version; leave that until someone
   asks, and say so in the docs.
5. **Tests**: making, moving and deleting a guide; a region snapping to one.

## Part 5 — The toolbar group, the help tip, the shortcuts sheet and the docs

1. **The group** in `src/components/toolbar/EditorToolbar.tsx` as described above, each button with a tooltip and
   `aria-pressed`.
2. **The help tip.** The first time an edge actually sticks (`emitHintEvent`, `src/lib/hint-events.ts`), a help tip
   points at the sticky-edges button (`data-hint-target`, a new `HintTargetId`) and explains: what sticks, that it
   lets go after a few pixels, Ctrl/Cmd to drag freely, and that the button turns it off. It obeys "Show help tips"
   (0.14.0 part 4). The rulers need no tip; their button's tooltip is enough.
3. **The shortcuts sheet** (`src/lib/shortcuts.ts`): `Ctrl/Cmd+R` if it was added, and guides (drag from a ruler,
   drag back to delete).
4. **Texts** in both languages; **docs**: `docs/controls.md` (sticky edges, rulers, guides), `docs/architecture.md`
   (the edge finder, sticky snapping, where guides live); **changelog**.

## Out of scope

- Guides saved in the project, guides at an angle, measuring between two points.
- Sticky edges for turned pieces.
- Snapping to the edges inside a grid's cells (the grid's own lines follow its rows and columns).

## Done when

On a clean PDF and on a scan, a region's edges stick to the pieces' borders and let go after a few pixels; the help
tip shows once, at the button; the rulers measure in mm and inches at any zoom with marks that follow the pointer;
guides are made, moved, deleted and snapped to; the switches remember their state; every check in `AGENTS.md`
passes.
